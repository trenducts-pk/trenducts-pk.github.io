Shoe Care in the Monsoon: Storing Shoes So They Survive the Rainy Season

Monsoon rains in Pakistan ruin shoes fast: mould, smells and peeling leather. Here is how to dry, clean and store shoes properly through July and August.

The monsoon is a hard season for shoes. Streets in Karachi, Lahore and Rawalpindi flood within minutes of heavy rain, and the walk from car to office door can soak a pair completely. Leather develops white mould spots, sports shoes smell, sandals lose their glue and closets full of shoes start to smell musty. Many families discover in September that their favourite Eid khussas or formal shoes have been ruined by humidity.

Proper drying, cleaning and storage can save your shoes, and your money. This guide explains how.

Step 01

Choose the right shoes for rain

Not every shoe suits the monsoon. On rainy days, wear rubber sandals, plastic slippers or waterproof shoes. Save leather shoes, suede, fabric khussas and expensive sneakers for dry days. Keep a pair of rain-friendly shoes at the office or in the car so you can change when you arrive.

Step 02

Dry wet shoes properly

When shoes get wet, do not put them in direct sunlight or next to a heater, which cracks leather and warps soles. Instead:

  • Remove insoles and laces
  • Stuff the shoes with newspaper, which absorbs water, and change it every few hours
  • Leave them in a ventilated spot with a fan blowing
  • Wipe leather with a dry cloth and apply a little conditioner once dry

Sports shoes can be washed gently and dried in shade with newspaper inside.

Step 03

Fight mould and smells

Mould loves damp, dark places. If white or green spots appear on leather, wipe them with a cloth dampened with diluted vinegar, then dry thoroughly. For smells, sprinkle baking soda inside shoes overnight and shake it out in the morning. Silica gel sachets, the small packets found in new shoe boxes, absorb moisture; save them and keep a few in each storage box.

Step 04

Storage that breathes

The way you store shoes matters as much as how you dry them. Closed plastic boxes trap moisture and encourage mould. Better options include racks with open or breathable fabric covers, shelves with gaps for air, and dustproof racks with roll-up curtains that keep dust out while letting air move. Step 05

Where to place the rack

Keep the shoe rack in a dry, ventilated spot, not in a damp corner by the bathroom or under a leaking window. If the entrance area gets wet, place a mat in front of the rack so wet shoes drip there first. Bamboo racks with a padded bench are a nice option for the entrance, giving a place to sit while removing wet shoes.

Step 06

Special care for festive shoes

Many families have khussas, heels and formal shoes worn only at weddings and Eid. Before the monsoon, clean them, let them dry, wrap them in cloth or tissue paper, and store them in breathable shoe bags with silica gel. Check them once a month during the rainy season. A little attention prevents ruined shoes when the wedding season returns in autumn.

Step 07

Weekly monsoon routine

During July and August, spend fifteen minutes a week on shoe care. Air out the shoe rack, wipe leather shoes, replace damp newspaper, check for mould spots and wash the floor mat. This small routine prevents most monsoon damage.

Step 08

Protect leather before the rains

Prevention is easier than repair. Before the monsoon begins in late June, clean your leather shoes and apply a good polish or a water-repellent spray. This creates a protective layer that keeps water from soaking straight in. Suede shoes need a special suede protector. Re-apply every few weeks during the rainy season. A ten-minute session at the start of the season can add years to a good pair of shoes.

Step 09

Keep a rain kit at the door

Place a small basket by the entrance with old newspapers, a dry cloth, a bottle of diluted vinegar and a few silica gel packs. When someone comes home with wet shoes, everything needed to dry and protect them is right there.

Step 10

Final thoughts

Shoes do not have to be a monsoon casualty. Wear rain-friendly pairs on wet days, dry soaked shoes slowly, fight mould and smells with simple home remedies and store everything in breathable racks. Your favourite pairs will come out of the rainy season looking as good as they went in.
