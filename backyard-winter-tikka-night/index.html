Backyard Winter Tikka Night: Planning a Charcoal BBQ at Home

A practical guide to planning a winter BBQ evening at home in Pakistan, from choosing a charcoal grill to marinades, timing and safety.

When the cold settles over Lahore, Islamabad or Peshawar in December, the smell of charcoal starts drifting over rooftops and lawns. Winter BBQ nights are one of the great pleasures of Pakistani family life: cousins gathered around a grill, chai on the side, everyone wrapped in shawls and arguing about whose turn it is to fan the coals.

You do not need a restaurant setup to host one. A decent grill, a little planning and good marinades are enough. Here is a practical plan.

Step 01

Pick the right kind of grill for your space

Grills come in a few broad styles, and each suits a different home.

  • Kettle grills with a lid trap heat, so they cook thicker pieces evenly and handle chilly, windy evenings better.
  • Large open charcoal grills with handles are made for big families and long skewer lines, and they are what most people picture for a tikka night.
  • Small countertop or tabletop grills suit a rooftop or a small courtyard and are perfect for two to four people.

Think about how many people you usually host and where you will store the grill for the rest of the year before choosing a size.

Step 02

Marinate the day before

The flavour of tikka and boti comes from time. Chicken does well with yoghurt, ginger-garlic paste, lemon, red chilli and your preferred tikka masala, left overnight in the fridge. Beef and mutton benefit from a raw papaya paste or yoghurt to tenderise, again ideally overnight. Paneer and vegetables need only an hour or two.

Keep marinated meat covered in the fridge, not on the counter, and take it out only shortly before grilling.

Step 03

Light the charcoal properly

Patience matters more than anything here. Light the charcoal and wait until it is covered in a thin layer of grey ash before you start cooking. Flames mean the coals are not ready yet; cooking over flames burns the outside while leaving the inside raw.

A chimney starter or a few firelighters make this easier than squirting kerosene, which leaves a smell on the meat. Keep a hand fan or a piece of cardboard nearby, because fanning is part of the tradition.

Step 04

Timing the evening

  • Light coals about forty minutes before you want to serve.
  • Start with vegetables and paneer, which cook quickly and keep hungry guests busy.
  • Move on to chicken tikka and boti, turning skewers regularly.
  • Finish with seekh kababs, which need steady medium heat.
  • Keep naan or paratha warm in foil near the edge of the grill.
Step 05

Safety on a cold night

Never bring a charcoal grill indoors or into a closed garage to escape the cold. Burning charcoal releases carbon monoxide, which is dangerous in enclosed spaces. Keep the grill in an open area, away from curtains, dry plants and children running around. Keep a bucket of water or sand nearby and let coals burn out completely before disposing of them.

Step 06

Sides that suit the season

Winter BBQ pairs well with mint raita, sliced onions with lemon, imli chutney and a big pot of chai or kahwa. A tray of roasted peanuts and chilgoza keeps people happy while the meat cooks.

Step 07

Cleaning up the next morning

Once the coals are completely cold, empty the ash into a metal bin or bag. Ash can hold heat for hours, so never tip it into a plastic dustbin the same night. Scrub the grate with a wire brush while it still has a little warmth from the morning sun, or soak it in hot soapy water. Wipe the outside of the grill and cover it before storing so dust and winter dew do not cause rust. A grill looked after this way lasts many seasons.

Step 09

The part that matters most

A winter tikka night is not really about the food, though the food should be good. It is about gathering people around warmth on a cold evening. Plan the marinade ahead, be patient with the coals, keep safety in mind, and the rest takes care of itself.
