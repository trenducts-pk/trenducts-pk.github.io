The Humble Serving Tray: How Pakistani Hosts Use Trays for Chai, Snacks and Dawats

A practical guide to serving trays: which types to own, how to style a tea tray for guests, tiered trays for parties and caring for wood and rattan.

Mehmaan nawazi, hospitality, is a point of pride in Pakistani culture. Guests are offered chai within minutes of arriving, followed by biscuits, nimko, samosas and whatever else is in the kitchen. And all of it arrives on a tray. The serving tray may be the most used item in a Pakistani home after the pateela, yet many of us do not give it much thought. The right trays make hosting easier and look much more welcoming.

  1. Why trays matter

    • They let you carry cups, plates and snacks in one trip
    • They protect tables from spills and hot cups
    • A well-styled tray makes even simple snacks look special
    • They keep a coffee table or side table neat between serving

  2. The trays every home should have

    A small collection covers almost every situation:

    • A medium rectangular tray with handles for chai and cups, easy to carry
    • A larger tray for dawats and serving several dishes or glasses
    • A tiered tray for parties, birthdays and Eid sweets
    • A decorative tray for the coffee table, holding candles, a vase or a tissue box

    Handles are important: a tray full of hot tea is heavy and slippery.

  3. Styling a tea tray for guests

    A neat tea tray creates a good first impression:

    • Line the tray with a small cloth or napkin to stop cups sliding
    • Arrange cups with handles facing the same direction
    • Add a sugar pot, a small milk jug and teaspoons
    • Put two small plates of snacks, one sweet and one savoury
    • Include napkins or a tissue box

  4. Tiered trays for parties

    Tiered trays save table space and look festive. Use them for mithai on Eid, cupcakes at birthdays, sandwiches and chaat at kitty parties, and dry fruit at weddings. Put heavier items on the bottom tier and lighter ones on top. They also look lovely as a centrepiece on the dining table.

  5. Materials

    • Wooden trays are warm and classic, ideal for chai and breakfast
    • Rattan and woven trays bring a natural, relaxed look
    • Metal and gold trays suit formal occasions and decor
    • Ceramic and porcelain trays match dinner sets

    Wood and rattan should not be soaked; wipe them with a damp cloth and dry immediately.

  6. Breakfast in bed and everyday uses

    Trays are not only for guests. A tray makes it easy to take breakfast to an elderly parent, bring medicines and water to a sick family member, or carry dinner to the lounge during a cricket match. A tray in the bathroom holds perfumes and skincare neatly.

  7. Caring for your trays

    Oil wooden trays occasionally with a little food-safe oil to stop them drying and cracking. Keep metal trays dry to avoid tarnish. Stack trays with a cloth between them.

  8. Trays as gifts

    A beautiful tray is a classic gift for a new home, a wedding or Eid. Fill it with dry fruit, mithai or a tea set to make a complete present.

  9. Quick questions

    • What size tray is best for chai? A medium tray of around 40 cm by 30 cm usually holds four to six cups with a sugar pot and a snack plate. Measure your cups if you are unsure.
    • How do I stop cups sliding on a tray? A small cloth, a rubber mat or a tray with a raised rim keeps cups steady while you walk.
    • Can wooden trays carry hot dishes? Yes, but place a trivet or folded napkin under very hot pots, as direct heat can mark the wood over time.
    • Is a tiered tray worth it for a small family? If you host Eid, birthdays or kitty parties, yes. It saves table space and doubles as a decorative centrepiece the rest of the time.
