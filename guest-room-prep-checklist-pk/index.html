Preparing the Guest Room: A Checklist for Hosting Relatives in Pakistani Homes

When relatives come to stay for a wedding, Eid or a long visit, a well-prepared guest room makes all the difference. Here is a practical checklist for Pakistani hosts.

Header photo: Wall-Mounted Storage Box - Punch-Free Phone & Remote Holder (Random Color) (photo: Trenducts.pk)

Hospitality runs deep in Pakistani culture. When relatives arrive from another city for a wedding, phupho comes from abroad for a month, or nanu visits for Eid, the family wants them to feel completely at home. Often the guest room doubles as a store room or a study for the rest of the year, so it takes some planning to turn it into a comfortable space.

This checklist covers what to do a week before, a day before and on arrival, plus small touches that guests notice and remember.

01

A week before: clear and clean

Start by clearing the room. Remove boxes, unused furniture and anything stored on the bed or in the cupboard. Guests need empty hanger space and at least one drawer for their clothes. Dust ceiling fans, light fixtures and the tops of cupboards, which collect dust quickly in Pakistani cities. Wash curtains if they have not been washed in a while. Check that the window locks and the bathroom door lock work properly.

02

Bedding and comfort

Comfort starts with the bed. Wash and air the mattress cover and pillows in the sun if possible. Use fresh, ironed bedsheets and pillowcases. Keep an extra blanket or razai in winter and a light cotton sheet in summer, since guests from other cities may feel the weather differently. If the guest is elderly, check that the bed height is comfortable to get in and out of.

03

Climate control

Pakistan's weather is extreme in both directions. In summer, check that the AC or air cooler works and has been serviced; the first switch-on of the season often reveals problems. Keep the remote in an easy-to-find holder on the wall. In winter, a small electric fan heater or gas heater makes the room cosy. For gas heaters, make sure the room is ventilated and explain safety to guests.

04

Small touches that matter

These details make guests feel truly welcome:

  • A wall holder or tray for phones, remotes and chargers
  • A multi-plug or extension board near the bed for charging devices
  • A key holder by the door with a spare house key
  • Fresh towels, a new soap and a toothbrush in case they forgot one
  • A prayer mat and a small Quran on a shelf, with the qibla direction marked
  • A jug of water and two glasses on the side table
  • The Wi-Fi password written on a small card

Many families pick up such home and living pieces in advance from places like the Trenducts home and living section, so the room is ready long before guests arrive.

05

The guest bathroom

If the guest room has an attached bathroom, give it extra attention. Clean it thoroughly, check that the geyser or water heater works, and keep the water bucket and lota clean. Stock toilet paper, hand wash, shampoo and a fresh towel. A small dustbin with a lid, a bath mat and a hook for clothes complete it.

06

Food and routines

Ask guests about dietary needs: elderly relatives may be diabetic or need low-salt food. Find out if they prefer tea in the morning or as soon as they wake up. Share household routines, such as when breakfast is served and when the main door is locked at night. Small conversations like these prevent awkward moments.

07

Respecting privacy

In busy Pakistani households, guests can feel they never have a moment alone. Let them rest after travel. Knock before entering their room. Give them space in the morning before chatting. Privacy makes a long stay comfortable for everyone.

08

A welcome note and a little local guide

Guests from another city often do not know the area. Leave a small note in the room with useful details: the nearest mosque and prayer timings, a pharmacy, a bakery, and the house phone numbers. If they plan to go shopping, mention the closest markets. This small effort helps them feel independent and saves them from asking for every little thing. Elderly guests especially appreciate knowing where things are without having to disturb the hosts.

09

Final thoughts

Preparing a guest room is a labour of love. Clear and clean in advance, focus on comfortable bedding and climate, add small thoughtful touches, prepare the bathroom and respect privacy. Your guests will leave feeling like family, and that is the heart of Pakistani mehmaan nawazi.
