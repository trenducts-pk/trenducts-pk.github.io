Beating Winter Dust and Smog: A Deep Cleaning Routine for Pakistani Homes

Dust storms, smog and construction make Pakistani homes dusty fast. A room-by-room deep cleaning routine with the right tools and less effort.

Updated October 2026

If you live in Lahore, Karachi, Multan or Islamabad, you know the feeling: you dust the drawing room in the morning, and by evening a fine layer is back on the table. Between dust storms, smog in October and November, construction next door and open windows in good weather, Pakistani homes collect dust quickly. Daily jharoo and pocha keep things presentable, but a proper deep clean once a season makes the home healthier and easier to maintain.

Plan the deep clean

Deep cleaning the whole house in one day is exhausting. Divide it over a weekend or a week, one or two rooms at a time. Start from the top of each room and work down: ceiling fans and corners first, then shelves, then furniture, and floors last. That way dust that falls is cleaned at the end. Open windows only when the air quality is reasonable; on heavy smog days, keep them closed and clean anyway.

The right tools save time

Good tools make cleaning faster and gentler on your back:

  • A flat mop with washable pads that reaches under beds and sofas
  • Microfibre cloths that trap dust instead of spreading it
  • A long-handled brush or duster for fans, curtain rods and cobwebs
  • A window wiper with a squeegee for glass and mirrors
  • A spray bottle for diluted cleaning solution

Replace old rags that leave lint behind. Microfibre cloths can be washed and reused for months.

Ceiling fans, lights and high corners

Ceiling fans in Pakistani homes run most of the year and gather thick dust on the blades. Switch off the fan, cover the bed or floor below with an old sheet, and wipe each blade with a damp cloth. A pillowcase slipped over the blade and pulled back traps most of the dust. Wipe light fittings and switchboards with a barely damp cloth, never a wet one. Clear cobwebs from corners with a long brush.

Windows, grills and jaalis

Windows and iron grills catch dust from the street. Wipe the grill with a damp cloth first, then clean the glass. A window wiper that sprays, scrubs and squeegees in one pass makes glass work quick and streak-free. Do not forget the window tracks: an old toothbrush and a vacuum or damp cloth removes the grit that makes sliding windows stiff. Wash curtains or at least shake them out on the roof and air them in the sun.

Floors and carpets

For marble, tiles and terrazzo floors, a flat mop with mildly soapy water is enough. Avoid flooding the floor; too much water leaves marks and takes long to dry. Move light furniture to clean under it at least once a season. Carpets and rugs should be vacuumed on both sides if possible, or taken to the roof and beaten in the sun, a time-tested method. Door mats at every entrance cut down how much dust comes in at all.

Kitchen and bathrooms

In the kitchen, dust mixes with oil and becomes sticky grime on top of cabinets, exhaust fans and the fridge. Use warm water with dishwashing liquid or a degreaser, and soak removable exhaust filters. In bathrooms, focus on tiles, taps and the area behind the commode. A stiff brush for grout lines makes a huge difference.

Keeping dust away afterwards

After the deep clean, small habits keep the home fresher longer. Dust with a microfibre cloth twice a week. Keep shoes at the door. Use door mats outside and inside. Keep windows closed on smog and dust storm days and air the house in the early morning when the air is cleaner. Indoor plants and an air purifier can help in bedrooms.

Bedrooms and soft furnishings

Mattresses, pillows and razais quietly collect dust mites. On a sunny day, carry them to the roof or balcony and let them air in the sun for a few hours, turning them once. Wash pillow covers and bedsheets in hot water, and vacuum or brush the mattress surface. Sofa cushions can be beaten gently outside to release trapped dust.
