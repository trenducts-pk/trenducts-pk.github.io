Winter Fish Fry at Home: Tools and Tips for Crispy Lahori-Style Machli

When winter arrives, fish season begins across Pakistan. Here is how to make crispy Lahori fish fry at home, with the right tongs, spatulas and frying tips.

In Pakistan, fish is a winter food. As soon as the weather turns cold in November, fish stalls on Lahore's roadsides and in famous spots near Mozang and Gawalmandi light up their karahis, and the smell of masala fish fills the evening air. In Karachi, families head to the fish markets for pomfret and surmai, and in the north, trout from the rivers is a seasonal treat.

Making fish fry at home is easier than many people think. With the right tools and a few tricks, you can get crisp, juicy pieces that rival the roadside stalls.

Step 01

Choosing the fish

Lahori fish fry is traditionally made with river fish like rahu or singhara, cut into boneless pieces. Sea fish such as surmai and pomfret work well too. Ask the fish seller to clean and cut it into medium pieces. Fresh fish has clear eyes, red gills and firm flesh that springs back when pressed. Avoid fish with a strong, unpleasant smell.

Step 02

Marinate for flavour

The secret of Lahori fish is a spicy, tangy marinade. Wash the fish well, then rub it with salt and lemon juice and leave for fifteen minutes to remove any fishy smell. Rinse and pat dry. Make a marinade of gram flour (besan), red chilli, turmeric, ajwain (carom seeds), ginger garlic paste, crushed coriander, salt and lemon juice. Coat the pieces well and rest them for at least an hour.

Step 03

The right tools

Frying fish needs a gentle touch, because pieces break easily. A few tools make a big difference:

  • Fish tongs with flat, wide grips lift pieces without breaking them
  • A slotted spatula or turner flips fish gently and drains oil
  • A silicone spatula for mixing marinade and scraping bowls clean
  • Kofta or meatball tongs, handy for fish kofta or fish balls, another winter favourite
  • A deep karahi or heavy pan that holds heat steadily

Stainless steel tongs are durable and easy to clean. You can find these and other cooking tools on the Trenducts tongs, whisks and spatulas page.

Step 04

Frying for crispiness

Heat oil in a karahi until it is hot but not smoking; a small drop of batter should sizzle and rise immediately. Fry a few pieces at a time so the oil temperature does not drop, which makes fish soggy and oily. Fry on medium-high heat until golden and crisp, turning gently with tongs. Drain on paper towels or a wire rack.

Step 05

Serving the Lahori way

Serve fish fry hot, sprinkled with chaat masala and a squeeze of lemon. Classic sides include mint chutney, imli chutney, sliced onions and naan. Many families enjoy it with a cup of green tea or kahwa afterwards, a combination often said to help digestion after fried food.

Step 06

Kitchen smell and safety

Frying fish leaves a strong smell. Open windows, switch on the exhaust and simmer a pot of water with lemon peels or cinnamon afterwards to freshen the air. Keep children away from hot oil. Never leave a hot karahi unattended, and never throw water on an oil fire; cover it with a lid instead.

Step 07

Leftovers and storage

Fried fish is best fresh, but leftovers can be reheated in an oven or air fryer to restore some crispness. Store marinated raw fish in airtight containers in the coldest part of the fridge and cook it within a day, or freeze it.

Step 08

Fish for health in winter

Fish is a good source of protein and healthy fats, and many families in Pakistan believe it keeps the body warm in cold months. To make it healthier, fry in fresh oil rather than reused oil, use less oil by shallow frying, or try baking or grilling marinated fish in the oven. Pair it with a fresh salad of cucumber, onion and tomato for a balanced winter meal.

Step 09

Final thoughts

Winter fish fry is one of the great pleasures of the Pakistani cold season. Choose fresh fish, marinate it well, use proper tongs and spatulas to protect the pieces, and fry in small batches. Serve with chutney and lemon, and you can bring the taste of Lahore's famous fish stalls to your own table. For more kitchen ideas, see the Trenducts.pk homepage.
