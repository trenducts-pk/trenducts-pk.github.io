Toddler Mealtimes Made Easier: Plates, Bibs and Habits for Little Eaters

Practical tips for feeding toddlers at home in Pakistan, from silicone plates and bibs to introducing family food and handling mess calmly.

Feeding a toddler is a daily adventure. One day they eat everything, the next they refuse their favourite dish and throw the spoon on the floor. In Pakistani homes, mealtimes are often shared with grandparents, aunts and cousins, each with their own advice about what and how a child should eat.

This guide offers practical ideas to make toddler mealtimes calmer and less messy. It is not medical or nutritional advice; for questions about your child's diet or growth, speak to your paediatrician.

Step 01

Let them sit with the family

Toddlers learn by watching. Whenever possible, seat your child at the dastarkhwan or dining table with everyone else, in a high chair or on a booster seat. Seeing adults eat roti, daal and vegetables encourages them to try the same foods. Family meals also make eating a social, happy time rather than a battle.

Step 02

Gear that genuinely helps

  • Silicone plates with suction bases stay put on the table, so they are harder to flip over.
  • Divided plates keep foods separate, which many toddlers prefer, and help you offer variety in small portions.
  • Soft-tipped spoons and small forks are easier for little hands and gentle on gums.
  • Silicone bibs with a pocket catch spills and are quick to rinse.
  • Squeeze feeders and training spoons help with purees for younger children moving to solids.

Choose items labelled food-grade and free of harmful chemicals, and check that they suit your child's age.

Step 03

Introducing family food

Many toddlers can gradually move from purees to soft versions of the family's food. Mashed daal with rice, khichdi, soft roti pieces dipped in yoghurt and well-cooked vegetables are common choices. Reduce chilli and salt for the child's portion, and take it out before adding the final tadka if needed. Always cut food into small pieces suitable for your child's age to reduce choking risk, and never leave a young child alone while eating.

Step 04

Let them self-feed, even if it is messy

Self-feeding helps toddlers develop coordination and independence. Yes, there will be daal on the floor and rice in their hair. A washable mat under the chair and a good bib make cleanup much easier. Offer a spoon even if they mostly use their hands; they will get the hang of it with time.

Step 05

Avoid pressure

It is tempting, especially with relatives watching, to insist that a child finishes everything. Pressure often backfires and makes children more resistant. Offer food, let them decide how much to eat, and try again another day with foods they reject. Many children need several tries before accepting a new taste.

Step 06

Keep a routine

Toddlers do well with regular meal and snack times. A predictable schedule helps them come to the table hungry rather than grazing all day. Limit sweet snacks and juices between meals so they have an appetite for proper food.

Step 07

Screen-free meals

Many parents use phones or cartoons to get a child to eat. It works in the moment, but children then eat without noticing what or how much. Try to keep meals screen-free, chatting with your child about the food instead. If you rely on screens now, reduce gradually.

Step 08

Cleaning and caring for feeding gear

Wash plates, bowls and spoons after every meal with warm soapy water. Many silicone items can be sterilised or washed in a dishwasher; check the instructions. Rinse bibs straight away before food dries. Replace items that become cracked or sticky.

Step 09

When grandparents help

Grandparents are often a huge help with feeding. Share your routine with them, and explain gently any changes, like using a divided plate or avoiding sugar. Most grandparents are happy to follow a plan when they understand the reason.

Step 11

Patience pays off
